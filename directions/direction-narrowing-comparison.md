## 1. Stagewise failure attribution as a standalone paper

### Research question and falsifiable hypothesis

- `[agent_inference]` Core research question: When coding-agent failures share the same normalized visible loop signature, do independently controlled evidence, derived-state, action, and validation interfaces reveal different causal bottlenecks that remain reproducible across scaffolds, models, and tasks? Source: `ideas/prior-art/IDEA_STAGEWISE_FAILURE_ATTRIBUTION.yaml / cross_field_attack.decision.exact_delta`, narrowed by the current comparison.
- `[agent_inference]` One-sentence hypothesis: Among deterministically restorable coding-agent failures with the same predeclared loop signature, single-interface oracle substitutions will produce repeatable and heterogeneous four-entry rescue profiles, and this heterogeneity will occur in more than one scaffold and model family. Source: prior delta plus the transfer and construct-validity gates in `directions/minimum-kill-pilot.md`.

### Closest work and exact novelty delta

- `[direct_evidence]` *Failure as a Process* annotates decisive-error, loss-of-recoverability, and observability times across 1,794 trajectories from three scaffolds and seven models, but labels its comparisons observational. Source: Zhao et al., arXiv:2607.09510v1, Sections II-E through III-C, Figure 4, Tables II-III, and Threats to Validity; `ideas/prior-art/IDEA_STAGEWISE_FAILURE_ATTRIBUTION.yaml / works.ARXIV_2607_09510`.
- `[direct_evidence]` Aegis localizes first failed subtasks, separates three failure families, and tests targeted environment optimizations, but does not independently replace the four proposed interfaces at one failure checkpoint. Source: Song et al., arXiv:2508.19504v1, Sections 3-6, Figure 10, and Table 3; stagewise prior-art ledger / `works.ARXIV_2508_19504`.
- `[direct_evidence]` AuditRepairBench holds the task, candidate set, tool outputs, and final evaluator fixed while blocking evaluator-derived selector inputs; its 80 source-level channel-surgery cases cover one validation-to-selection path and explicitly do not certify a latent mechanism or strong prospective validity. Source: Hu et al., arXiv:2605.04624v1, Sections 1, 3, 6, and 13.
- `[direct_evidence]` Interchange interventions, dynamic mediation, sequential actual causality, and counterfactual credit already provide formal intervention and effect-decomposition machinery. Source: Geiger et al., JMLR 26 (2025), Definitions 44-48 and 52; Ge et al., ICML 2023, Sections 3-4; Triantafyllou et al., AIES 2022, Sections 2.2-3 and 5; Schug et al., NeurIPS 2023, Sections 3-5.
- `[agent_inference]` Exact novelty delta: the paper may claim only causal evidence that externally typed, executable code-agent interfaces yield reproducible rescue-profile heterogeneity for observationally matched loops, with replication beyond one scaffold and model. It may not claim a new causal estimator, mediation definition, interchange intervention, failure taxonomy, or recovery-window concept. Source: closest-work boundaries above; `directions/novelty-boundary.md / Stagewise finalist`.

### Contribution type

- `[agent_inference]` Primary contribution: `new_causal_evidence`. Conditional secondary contribution: `new_phenomenon` if matched loop signatures are demonstrably non-identifying and the rescue profiles repeat across configurations. Source: stagewise prior-art ledger / `cross_field_attack.transfer_assessment.residual_contribution` and the current hypothesis.
- `[agent_inference]` There is no defensible `new_method` contribution at narrowing time. The intervention operators are applications of mature causal machinery, while the empirical interface decomposition and resulting evidence are the proposed SE contribution. Source: Geiger et al. and Ge et al. locators above.

### What executable code structure contributes

- `[agent_inference]` Code tasks provide a jointly inspectable tuple that is unusually practical to intervene on: a versioned repository and filesystem state, replayable tool or test observations, an explicit working-state or diagnosis payload, an executable patch/tool action, and a broader behavioral validator. Source: `synthesis/cross-paper-synthesis.md / P2, P5, and P7`; stagewise ledger / `transfer_assessment.code_specific_structure`.
- `[agent_inference]` This structure enables byte- or semantic-level checkpoint checks, executable action substitution, and validation against behavior not shown to the agent. These are stronger construct checks than latent-state-only mediation. Source: same sources; `directions/minimum-kill-pilot.md / Executable checkpoint`.
- `[agent_inference]` The environment is not theoretically unique: a sufficiently instrumented simulator can expose the same interfaces. The SE contribution therefore depends on an observed code-agent phenomenon and on artifact-level auditability, not on claiming that only software can be checkpointed. Source: stagewise ledger / `general_environment_substitutability`.

### Cross-scaffold, model, and task validity

- `[agent_inference]` The interface vocabulary is scaffold-compatible only if adapters expose the same intervention contract without equating scaffold-specific prompt fields. The pilot must therefore use two independently implemented open scaffolds rather than two configurations of one harness. Source: current hypothesis; SEAlign's negative scaffold-transfer evidence in `synthesis/cross-paper-synthesis.md / P9`.
- `[agent_inference]` Model validity requires at least one open-weight or locally pinned model and one independent API/model family; task validity requires multiple repositories and at least two failure-producing task families. A result confined to one cell is a harness diagnosis, not the finalist contribution. Source: `directions/minimum-kill-pilot.md / Minimum scale and transfer gate`.

### Positive and negative result value

- `[agent_inference]` Positive result: stable within-checkpoint rescue profiles plus within-signature causal heterogeneity would show that visible loops are non-identifying symptoms and establish a causal measurement target for choosing where recovery support is needed. Source: hypothesis and pilot continuation gate.
- `[agent_inference]` Negative result A: if ordinary error counts or trace labels identify the same bottleneck, the study would support simpler observational diagnostics and kill the stagewise contribution. Source: idea card / `minimum_kill_validation.kill_criterion`.
- `[agent_inference]` Negative result B: if all four oracle substitutions act alike, the four-stage decomposition lacks empirical resolution; the conclusion would be a common downstream bottleneck or intervention leakage, not stagewise attribution. Source: pilot separability gate.
- `[agent_inference]` Negative result C: if rescue profiles are stable only within one scaffold or model, the result supports a local debugging instrument but not the finalist's transferable phenomenon claim. Source: transfer gate below.

### Most likely top-venue rejection

- `[agent_inference]` The most likely rejection is "mature causal intervention applied to SWE-bench with artificial gold oracles." This rejection is warranted unless each substitution is independently valid, off-target effects are measured, alternative valid fixes are handled, and the result transfers beyond one harness. Source: causal-method coverage above; stagewise ledger / `reviewer_rejection` and `unverified_information`.
- `[agent_inference]` A second rejection is construct arbitrariness: the four interfaces may be researcher-chosen abstractions rather than isolated mediators, especially when evidence changes state and validation changes later actions. Source: Ge et al., ICML 2023, Sections 2-4; longitudinal identification warning in Bind et al., *Biostatistics* 2016, Sections 2-5.

### Verdict

- `[agent_inference]` Retain as the only finalist, under the exact name `FINALIST_STAGEWISE_CAUSAL_RESCUE_PROFILES`. It advances only if the minimum pilot passes every engineering and scientific continuation gate; no method or novelty-first claim is pre-approved. Source: the analysis above and `directions/minimum-kill-pilot.md`.

## 2. Counterfactual evidence memory audit as a standalone paper

### Research question and falsifiable hypothesis

- `[agent_inference]` Strongest remaining research question: Does response-probability or deletion-based attribution misidentify a code-agent memory item's behavioral value when the item's truth is bound to an earlier repository snapshot? Source: memory prior-art ledger / `cross_field_attack.decision.exact_delta`.
- `[agent_inference]` Strongest one-sentence hypothesis: Rankings from standard context ablation will disagree materially with matched remove, program-false corruption, and valid-restore effects on next executable action and hidden behavioral correctness for snapshot-stale memory items. Source: memory ledger / `minimum_pilot`.

### Closest work and exact novelty delta after the targeted update

- `[direct_evidence]` Removal, inclusion, Shapley-style interaction effects, necessity, sufficiency, plausible replacement, and response-level context attribution are already formalized and evaluated. Source: Covert et al., JMLR 2021, Definitions 1-3 and Sections 3-6; Watson et al., UAI 2021 / *Minds and Machines* 2022, Sections 2-4; DeYoung et al., ACL 2020, Section 4.2; Balkir et al., NAACL 2022, Sections 3-5; ContextCite, arXiv:2409.00729v2, Sections 2-5 and Appendix C.4.
- `[direct_evidence]` Truth-maintenance systems record justifications, trace assertions to assumptions, retract dependent assertions, retain alternative justifications, and represent multiple assumption contexts. Source: Shapiro, *Belief Revision and Truth Maintenance Systems: An Overview and a Proposal*, Technical Report 98-10, Sections 1, 3, and 6.
- `[direct_evidence]` Belief-update theory explicitly distinguishes learning new information about one world state from a changed world and uses time-indexed propositions for the latter. Source: Hansson, *Logic of Belief Revision*, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Section 6.1, summarizing Katsuno and Mendelzon (1992), DOI:10.1017/CBO9780511526664.007.
- `[direct_evidence]` MPBench studies four memory-write channels, nine vulnerabilities, six poisoning classes, persistent writes, and cross-session action influence on two agent systems; it reports average attack-write success of 50.46% and retrieval-stage behavioral success of 41.05%. Source: Dash et al., arXiv:2606.04329v2, Abstract, Sections 2-4, and Table 2.
- `[direct_evidence]` MemLineage attaches signed provenance and derivation lineage to entries, retains deletion tombstones, propagates untrusted ancestry, gates sensitive actions, and evaluates strip/repair recovery and utility on deterministic and AgentDojo harnesses. Source: Ouyang and Hou, arXiv:2605.14421v1, Sections 3.3-3.7, 6, 7, and 8.
- `[agent_inference]` After these checks, no standalone novelty delta is strong enough: combining version-indexed belief update, dependency lineage, removal attribution, corruption injection, executable action scoring, and hidden tests is a software instantiation and benchmark protocol, not a new phenomenon established in advance or a new method. Source: all closest-work boundaries above.

### Contribution type

- `[agent_inference]` Current contribution class: `only_application_transfer`. A future surprising empirical failure of standard attribution could create an applicability-boundary contribution, but absence of that result at narrowing time cannot be used as novelty evidence. Source: `AGENTS.md / Evidence and Claims`; memory ledger / `residual_contribution`, revised by the targeted works above.
- `[agent_inference]` The original memory-control method is also covered in substance by Auto-Dreamer, Memory-R1, A-MemGuard, and current provenance defenses. Source: memory ledger / direct-stage `works` and `decision`; MemLineage Sections 7.5 and 7.9.

### What executable code structure contributes

- `[agent_inference]` Code offers useful measurement conveniences: repository-version provenance, executable fact checks, deterministic diffs, and hidden behavioral tests. Source: memory ledger / `code_specific_structure`.
- `[agent_inference]` Those conveniences are substitutable by versioned databases, web environments, workflow engines, or simulators with time-indexed facts, transactions, provenance, and outcome oracles. The targeted truth-maintenance and memory-security work removes the argument that snapshot provenance plus downstream actions is intrinsically code-specific. Source: Shapiro Sections 1-6; MPBench Sections 2-4; MemLineage Sections 2-3.

### Cross-scaffold, model, and task validity

- `[agent_inference]` The underlying phenomenon should generalize, but that generality weakens rather than strengthens a standalone SE delta because the proposal does not identify a program-semantic property absent from other persistent agents. Source: substitutability assessment above.
- `[agent_inference]` Policy dependence and multi-item interactions would require several memory managers, models, and task domains; a one-scaffold result would be indistinguishable from a retrieval-policy artifact. Source: ContextCite Appendix C.4; memory ledger / `unverified_information`.

### Positive and negative result value

- `[agent_inference]` Positive result: standard deletion attribution fails under temporal state change and dual behavioral endpoints. This could motivate a later, newly narrowed applicability-boundary seed, but under the current framing it would still combine known belief-update and attribution concepts. Source: closest-work synthesis above.
- `[agent_inference]` Negative result: ContextCite-style ablation or lineage scores already predict executable action and hidden outcomes, confirming that code adds no new audit principle. Source: memory ledger / `minimum_pilot.kill_criterion`; MemLineage Sections 3.5-3.7.
- `[agent_inference]` Interaction-dominated or unstable effects would show that per-item causal memory scores are not a valid construct, independently killing the proposed audit. Source: Covert et al., Sections 5-6; ContextCite Appendix C.4.

### Most likely top-venue rejection and verdict

- `[agent_inference]` The most likely rejection is "ContextCite plus belief update/TMS plus a memory-poison benchmark on coding tasks." A second rejection is that hidden tests still do not adjudicate user intent or alternative valid fixes. Source: novelty synthesis above; `synthesis/cross-paper-synthesis.md / P7`.
- `[agent_inference]` Verdict: `KILL`. Do not allocate a pilot in the next phase. The pressure point may be reseeded only if a program-semantic property creates a qualitative attribution failure that is not reproducible in another versioned agent environment. Source: current application-transfer decision.

## 3. Unified code-agent causal trajectory auditing

### Research question and falsifiable hypothesis

- `[agent_inference]` Strongest possible umbrella question: Can executable causal interventions jointly identify which trajectory interface blocks recovery and which individual state facts remain valid and necessary as code evolves? Source: combination of the two residual questions above.
- `[agent_inference]` Strongest one-sentence hypothesis: A common intervention framework will yield stable four-interface rescue profiles and, within the state interface, stable item-level necessity profiles that jointly predict hidden behavioral correctness across scaffolds. Source: combined proposal as charitably reconstructed for this comparison.
- `[agent_inference]` This is a conjunction of two independently killable hypotheses, not one shared causal claim. Stagewise rescue asks which external interface changes recovery; memory attribution asks whether item-removal scores remain valid under temporal truth change. Source: each direction's intervention object and endpoint above.

### Novelty delta and contribution type

- `[agent_inference]` No extra novelty delta arises from using one checkpoint engine or the phrase "causal trajectory auditing." Interchange intervention can host both experiments, but shared instrumentation is not a shared research contribution. Source: Geiger et al., Definitions 44-48; the distinct estimands above.
- `[agent_inference]` Contribution class: a bundle of one potentially new empirical phenomenon and one application transfer. More experimental cells do not convert that bundle into a new method or stronger phenomenon. Source: individual direction decisions.

### Code structure, transfer, and result interpretation

- `[agent_inference]` The combined system would reuse repository snapshots, tool-output injection, state replacement, patch execution, provenance, and hidden tests. This reduces engineering duplication but does not resolve the different oracle-validity requirements. Source: both ledgers / `code_specific_structure`; `directions/minimum-kill-pilot.md`.
- `[agent_inference]` Cross-scaffold and cross-model validity becomes harder: every configuration must expose four stage interfaces and item-level provenance, so missing memory hooks would conflate scaffold coverage with the scientific hypothesis. Source: stagewise adapter requirement and memory policy dependence above.
- `[agent_inference]` A positive stagewise result with a negative memory result would support only the standalone finalist; a positive memory result with a negative stagewise result would support a newly reseeded memory boundary, not the umbrella; two positive results would still require two separate novelty arguments. Source: hypothesis decomposition above.
- `[agent_inference]` A joint negative result would reject both constructs but would not produce a coherent positive contribution beyond an engineering caution about causal replay. Source: individual negative interpretations.

### Most likely top-venue rejection and verdict

- `[agent_inference]` The most likely rejection is an over-broad benchmark/tool paper that combines mature causal operators without a single estimand, and whose conclusions depend on two different incomplete oracles. Source: cross-field method coverage and P7 intent boundary.
- `[agent_inference]` Verdict: `REJECT_MERGE`. Keep memory interventions available only as a possible state-stage stress control inside the stagewise pilot; do not elevate them to a second contribution or require them for the finalist claim. Source: portfolio decision.
